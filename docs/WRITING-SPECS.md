# 📝 How to Write Specs for BOT

## A Guide for Agatha (and anyone helping!)

---

## What is a Spec?

A **spec** (short for "specification") is a document that explains what you want BOT to do. It's like writing instructions for BOT's personality!

Think of it like this:
- A recipe tells someone how to make a cake
- A spec tells BOT how to be your robot friend

---

## The Most Important Part: If-Then Rules

The heart of BOT's personality is the **If-Then Rules** table. It works like this:

> **IF** someone says something → **THEN** BOT does something

### Example:

| When someone says... | BOT should... |
|---------------------|---------------|
| "Hello!" | Say "Hi there! I'm so happy to see you!" |

This means:
- **IF** someone says "Hello!"
- **THEN** BOT says "Hi there! I'm so happy to see you!"

### Writing Good If-Then Rules

**Do:**
- ✅ Be specific about what triggers the rule
- ✅ Write exactly what BOT should say (in quotes)
- ✅ Think about how it would feel to hear BOT's response

**Don't:**
- ❌ Make rules too complicated
- ❌ Forget about feelings (how will this make someone feel?)
- ❌ Write mean or scary responses

### Example: A Good Rule vs. Not-So-Good Rule

**Not-so-good:**
| When someone says... | BOT should... |
|---------------------|---------------|
| Something | Say something back |

**Good:**
| When someone says... | BOT should... |
|---------------------|---------------|
| "I had a bad day" | Say "I'm sorry to hear that. Want to tell me about it, or would you like me to try to cheer you up?" |

The good rule is specific and kind!

---

## The No-No List

The **No-No List** tells BOT what it should NEVER do. This keeps BOT safe and friendly.

### Example No-No List:
- 🚫 Never be mean or sarcastic
- 🚫 Never say scary things
- 🚫 Never ignore someone who's sad
- 🚫 Never use bad words

### Why is the No-No List Important?

Even smart robots can make mistakes. The No-No List helps BOT know the boundaries - just like how you know certain things are not okay to do!

---

## Approved Content Lists

Sometimes BOT needs a list of things it's allowed to say or do. For example:

### Approved Jokes List

Instead of letting BOT make up any joke (which might not be funny or appropriate), you give BOT a list of jokes YOU picked:

1. Why don't scientists trust atoms? Because they make up everything!
2. What do you call a fake noodle? An impasta!

This way, you know BOT will only tell jokes you've approved!

## How to Edit the Spec

### On GitHub (Online)

1. Go to your repository on GitHub
2. Click on the `specs` folder
3. Click on `bot-personality.md`
4. Click the ✏️ pencil icon to edit
5. Make your changes
6. Scroll down and write what you changed
7. Click "Commit changes"

### On Your Computer

1. Open the file in a text editor (like Typora or VS Code)
2. Make your changes
3. Save the file
4. Tell your parent you're ready to test!

## Tips for Writing Great Specs

### 1. Think Like BOT's Friend
Ask yourself: "If I was talking to BOT, what would I want to hear?"

### 2. Start Small
You don't have to write everything at once! Start with:
- 3 greetings
- 3 emotional responses
- 3 jokes

### 3. Test Your Ideas
After writing new rules, test them! Talk to BOT and see if it responds the way you expected.

### 4. Keep a Change Log
At the bottom of your spec, write down what you changed and when. This helps you remember your ideas!

| Date | What I Changed |
|------|---------------|
| Jan 15 | Added 3 new jokes |
| Jan 20 | Made sad response kinder |

### 5. Have Fun!
This is YOUR robot friend. Make BOT exactly how you want!
